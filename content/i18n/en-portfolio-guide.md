# English portfolio translation guide

## Brief

- **Audience:** international freelance clients, hiring teams, and corporate training or hackathon program managers.
- **Purpose:** show what Fullkeem builds, how he helps project teams move, and how to start a conversation.
- **Voice:** professional, direct, warm, and specific. Prefer short active sentences over marketing superlatives.
- **Accuracy:** do not invent outcomes, client names, metrics, seniority, or ownership that the Korean source does not support.

## Approved terminology

| Korean source | English | Usage note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 외주 | freelance project / client project | Avoid “outsourcing.” |
| 기업교육 | corporate training program | Use “corporate training” in short UI labels. |
| 보조강사 | teaching assistant | Do not upgrade to instructor or lead trainer. |
| 멘토 | mentor | Use “project execution mentor” when the focus is delivery. |
| PBL | project-based learning (PBL) | Spell out on first use in body copy. |
| AX | AI transformation (AX) | Spell out for an international audience; use AX only after context. |
| 배포 | deploy / ship | “Ship” is acceptable in headlines; “deploy” is clearer in body copy. |
| 실무의 고민 | real workplace problem | Avoid a literal translation such as “work concern.” |

Product names—MagPlus, Shoong!, Seoullette, At the Place, and Seojae-check—stay unchanged.

## Style rules

- Use American English.
- Lead with a concrete role or outcome; explain the method in the next sentence.
- Use first person on the homepage and resume. Use neutral product language in case studies.
- Keep headlines bold, but keep body copy literal and verifiable.
- Avoid “innovative,” “seamless,” “cutting-edge,” “expert,” and unsupported claims such as “production-ready.”
- CTA labels should describe the next step: “See selected work,” “Review my resume,” or “Discuss a project.”
